Learning-based network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) achieve strong detection performance, yet their decision processes often remain opaque, which limits both the trust that can be placed in them and the extent to which their failures can be diagnosed. This manuscript, currently in preparation, presents a systematic review of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) techniques applied to NIDS.

The review addresses three central questions:

- **Interpretability:** To what extent do existing XAI methods yield explanations that are faithful to the underlying model and meaningful to security analysts?
- **Trustworthiness:** How are the reliability, stability, and consistency of explanations assessed in the literature?
- **Robustness:** Can explanation-based approaches contribute to the detection of, and reasoning about, previously unseen (zero-day) attacks?

By consolidating methodological approaches, evaluation practices, and open problems, this work aims to provide a structured foundation for future research on transparent and verifiable intrusion detection.
