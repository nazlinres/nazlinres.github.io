## Preface: Why Independent Research?

Institutional academia shapes knowledge production within a specific hierarchy, funding mechanism, and publication pressure. This structure often rewards staying within existing paradigms rather than asking original questions. I have chosen to work as an **independent researcher** outside this structure. This is not an escape; it is a deliberate preference for a direct, unmediated, and open-access path to knowledge.

## Research Areas and Theoretical Framework

### 1. Explainable AI and Network Intrusion Detection Systems (XAI for NIDS)

Deep learning models achieve high accuracy in network intrusion detection. However, their decision mechanisms are not interpretable due to their **high-dimensional and non-linear** nature. This weakens security analysts' trust in model outputs and delays the adoption of these systems in critical infrastructures.

My theoretical framework is built on how methods such as **Shapley additive explanations (SHAP)**, **local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME)**, and **integrated gradients** can be interpreted in the feature space of network traffic. In particular, in the detection of **zero-day attacks**, explaining *why* a model made a decision is not merely a technical requirement; it is an epistemological necessity.

### 2. Container and Cloud-Native Security: Theoretical Analysis of Syscall Sequences

Container technologies are built on a shared kernel model. While this architecture provides resource efficiency, it also raises the question of how reliable **isolation boundaries** are in theory. Container escape attacks occur when these boundaries are violated.

My focus is on detecting deviations from normal behavior through the statistical and structural analysis of **system call (syscall) sequences**. N-gram models, LSTM networks, and Transformer architectures can be used to model these sequences as **time series**. However, the real theoretical question is: **Can the "normal" behavior of a container be defined universally?** Or should each container be evaluated within its own context?

### 3. Human-Centered Cybersecurity: Cognitive Biases and Phishing Detection

Cybersecurity is not only a technical problem; it is also a **cognitive and behavioral** one. Phishing attacks target weaknesses in human decision-making. AI-generated phishing emails have become indistinguishable from real ones in linguistic and visual terms.

My theoretical framework in this area is built on **cognitive biases**, **heuristic decision-making**, and **attention economy** concepts. My aim is to model under which situational and individual factors users become more vulnerable, and to transform these models into defense strategies.

## Goals: From Theory to Practice, From Earth to Space

### Short Term (0 to 2 years)

- Publish my first systematic review on an **open-access** platform
- Develop an open-source Python library for syscall-based anomaly detection
- Produce video content with theoretical depth and contribute to the open-access knowledge network

### Mid Term (2 to 5 years)

- Present a paper at an international conference as an **independent researcher**
- Establish collaborations with researchers in my field without hierarchy
- Complete my doctoral education, but **by following my own questions**

### Long Term (5 to 15 years)

- Establish my independent research identity at an international level
- Carry cybersecurity knowledge to **places where money is not**
- Mentor young researchers, encouraging them not to conform to the system, but to **question it**

### Radical Vision (15+ years)

- **Go to space.** Yes, I am serious. Humanity's next leap is beyond Earth. Cybersecurity will play a critical role in space systems as well. Satellite communication, ground stations, and orbital systems are all vulnerable. I want to work in this area and contribute to the security of spacecraft and communication systems.
- **Found an independent research institute.** An open-access, hierarchy-free knowledge production center as an alternative to institutional academia.
- **Carry cybersecurity education to regions with limited access.** Knowledge knows no borders.

## Final Word: Knowledge and Freedom

Institutions fall, titles fade, citation counts are forgotten. What remains is **the knowledge you produce** and **the people you touch**.

I do not belong to an institution. I belong to an **idea**: Knowledge liberates.

*"It's only after we've lost everything that we're free to do anything."*
